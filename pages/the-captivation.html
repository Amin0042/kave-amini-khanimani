Motion Graphics · Case Study

The Captivation

►

Introduction

The Captivation is a 40-second abstract motion piece dedicated to the innocent people of Iran, held captive under a totalitarian regime for more than four decades. A gold map with legs, red bars, and a Persian carpet move inside the outline of the country. It was my second completed project in Blender, finished in Adobe After Effects.

  • Project type: Animated Short, Motion Graphics 3 (DSN1688), Algonquin College, Fall 2026
  • Role: Concept, asset customization, scene design, animation, and compositing
  • Programs: Blender, Adobe After Effects
  • 3D assets: Downloaded from Sketchfab and customized for this project

The Challenge

The brief asked for a 30-second animated short that tells a story or conveys a message, delivered at 1920 × 1080. The project moved from planning to an animatic and then a finished animation, with attention to timing, pacing, and clean transitions.

My challenge was to express captivity through movement. I wanted the country to feel like a vulnerable body, with its people and culture held inside a restricted space.

The Process

Building. I downloaded the assets from Sketchfab and customized them in Blender. I brought the map, legs, bars, and carpet into one scene, arranging the objects and their movement around the idea of captivity.

Rendering. I rendered the animation as a series of 4K images. The sequence took more than ten hours to render. Those frames became the source material for the final composition.

Finishing. In After Effects, I assembled the image sequence and finished the animation. For the public YouTube version shown here, I masked everything into the shape of Iran and made a 40-second edit.

The Solution

The inner gold map has legs, giving the country a human presence. Red bars obstruct the view, while the Persian carpet changes from a ground beneath the figure into a pattern that fills much of the outer map. The scene moves, but the enclosing outline stays fixed.

That outer mask makes containment part of every shot. Gold connects the map and lettering, red gives the bars a sharp presence, and black keeps the country's border clear. The carpet carries a recognisable part of Iranian culture through the sequence.

The Outcome

The featured result is the 40-second YouTube adaptation, with its Iran-shaped mask. The course brief specified a separate 30-second delivery.

Reflection & Learning

Skills I strengthened: customizing existing assets, arranging and animating a Blender scene, rendering image sequences, and finishing them in After Effects.

As a beginner on my second Blender project, using existing models let me concentrate on movement and meaning. The most important choice was the outer mask: it connects the changing images to the dedication throughout the piece.
